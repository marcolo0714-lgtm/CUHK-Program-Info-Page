Major Programme Requirement

 

Studies in Fine Arts are divided into THREE main streams:

(1)   Art History – studies the historical development of art with a special emphasis on China;

(2)   Studio Art – trains students in the practice of artistic creation; and

(3)   Art History and Studio Art – applies equal emphasis on both.

Students must declare one of the streams at the beginning of their third year of attendance.

Study Scheme
Learning Outcomes
 
FAAS

1.          Major programme:

Upon completion of the programme, students will:

1.      have gained an understanding of the development of Chinese art and Western art (K/V);

2.      have acquired the ability to approach and appreciate art in a cultural perspective (K/S/V);

3.      be informed of the multi-cultural environment pertaining to the development of art in Hong Kong (K/S/V);

4.      have acquired the ability to demonstrate artistic skills in a variety of media (K/S);

5.      have acquired the ability to apply both traditional and modern art concepts in their artistic works (K/S);

6.      be academically prepared for either a professional career or further studies (K/S/V);  and

7.       be able to embrace art as a way of life (V).

2.          Minor programme:

i.      Fine Arts

Upon the completion of the Minor programme, students will have demonstrated basic understanding of fundamental issues related to the subject, acquired knowledge of some aspects of the subject with sufficient exposure and at a suitable level to allow further study in this subject or related subjects or to pursue a related career.

 

ii.       Curating and Artistic Strategies

Upon completion of the Minor programme, students will have acquired the ability to:

 

I.        Knowledge outcomes:

·         Demonstrate an understanding of the development of contemporary art internationally and locally from mid-20th to early 21st Century and the creative strategies adopted by artists

·         Explain key historical and contemporary examples of exhibition design andcurating

·         Explain the key roles of being a curator

·         Demonstrate analytical skills concerning exhibition experiences, audience engagement and sociability

 

II    Skills outcomes:

·         Develop skills in the various stages of exhibition-making from a broad spectrum of real world experience and professional networking opportunities

·         Demonstrate skills in idea conceptualization, proposal writing, pitching, budgeting, contracting, publication, exhibition design, installation and exhibition maintenance

·         Demonstrate artistic skills in photographic, textual and image productions

 

III   Attitude outcomes:

·         Acquire personal and socialcompetencies to transfercurating into knowledgesharing and act as an active agent in facilitating knowledge engagement

·         Acquire personal and social competencies to work cooperatively in a team

·         Develop an attitude of innovation and creativity by challenging assumptions in the ever expanding fields of artistic production and curation

·         Develop cross-disciplinary work attitude between arts management and creative art practice
