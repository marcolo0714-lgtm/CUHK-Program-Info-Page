Study Scheme
Learning Outcomes
 
RELS

1.

Major programme:

 

Knowledge:

Graduates will have acquired the following:

 

(1)

The understanding of religion in its historical settings and contemporary contexts of modern living, globally and locally.

 

(2)

The understanding of religion in its different dimensions.

 

(3)

The understanding of religion through a crtical analysis.

 

(4)

A basic knowledge of major religious traditions in the world.

 

Skills:

Graduates will have acquired the following:

 

(1)

Communication and presentational (oral and written) skills.

 

(2)

Analytical and problem solving skills.

 

(3)

Social skills and team-work skills.

 

(4)

Skills of critical intelligence and appreciative thinking.

 

(5)

Skills in cross-cultural understanding and intercultural literacy.

 

(6)

Research skills.

 

Attitude:

Graduates will have acquired the following:

 

(1)

Awareness of the plurality and multi-faceted complexity within religious traditions of the issues of multiple and conflicting interpretations of religious traditions.

 

(2)

Self-critical awareness of pre-suppositions and recognition.

 

(3)

A critical and yet sympathetic understanding and appreciation of religious traditions especially those to which one does not belong.

 

(4)

Sensitivity to the ultimate concern and the religious dimension of human life.

 

 

 

 

2.

Minor programme:

 

i.

Arab Studies

 

Upon completion of the programme, students will have acquired the following learning outcomes in three aspects:

 

I.        Knowledge outcomes:

(1)     Understand religions and cultures of the Arab World in their historical settings and contemporary contexts of modern living.

(2)     Recognize central themes and issues in the Arab World, especially with respect to cultural and religious dimensions.

 

II.       Skills outcomes:

(1)     Basic command of the Arabic language.

(2)     Engage in a critical analysis of issues pertaining to the Arab World by the methods of the humanities and social sciences.

 

III.     Attitude outcomes:

(1)     Have awareness of plurality and multi-faceted complexity within Arab cultural, political and religious traditions, and of the issues of multiple and conflicting interpretations of language and symbols, texts and traditions.

(2)     Acknowledge the contested and provisional nature of knowledge and understanding. Show tolerance of diverse positions and have respect for others’ views.

 

 

ii.

Religious Studies

 

Upon the completion of the Minor programme, students will have demonstrated basic understanding of fundamental issues related to the subject, acquired knowledge of some aspects of the subject with sufficient exposure and at a suitable level to allow further study in this subject or related subjects or to pursue a related career.

 

 

iii.     Religion and Health

Upon completion of this Minor Programme, students will have acquired the ability to:

 

I. Knowledge outcomes:

a)      gain a comprehensive understanding of the relationship between religion and health, including the impact of religion (e.g. belief/ritual/practice) on physical, mental, and spiritual well-being, within their cultural and historical contexts.

b)      analyze how religion influences health behaviours, healthcare decision-making, and attitudes towards illness and healing.

c)      identify and utilize resources and practices that promote spiritually informed self-care from diverse perspectives.

 

II. Skills outcomes:

a)      design health programmes that incorporate religious resources, such as texts, rituals, or practices, to enhance the well-being of individuals and communities.

b)      demonstrate proficiency in conducting research and data analysis related to religion and health.

c)      effectively communicate findings to diverse audiences, including healthcare professionals, community stakeholders and policymakers.

 

III. Values and attitude outcomes:

a)      foster cultural sensitivity and respect for the clients’ religious beliefs and practices in healthcare settings.

b)      increase awareness of the impact of religious resources on population health in different parts of our global communities.

c)      engage in ongoing education and dialogue to deepen understanding of the intersection between spirituality, healthcare ethics, and diverse religious traditions.
