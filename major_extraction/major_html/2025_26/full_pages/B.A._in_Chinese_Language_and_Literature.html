Study Scheme
Learning Outcomes
 
CHLL

1.

Major Programme:

 

Upon successful completion of this Programme, students will (i) have acquired expertise in four primary fields in Chinese studies, namely, Chinese language and linguistics, classical Chinese texts, classical Chinese literature and modern Chinese literature; (ii) have acquired effective learning strategies and skills pertinent to Chinese studies and lifelong self-education; (iii) have enhanced their critical, intellectual and aesthetic sense and sensibilities; and (iv) have understood the cultural and social values that have shaped the contemporary world.

 

 

2.

Chinese Language and Literature and Translation Double Major Programme:

 

Upon completing the Programme, students will have acquired the ability:

(1)

to have a general understanding of Chinese language in everyday life and in ancient time, and develop sustained interest in further pursuing some of the linguistic issues; (K/V)

(2)

to apply digital tools and databases in learning; (K/S)

(3)

to show creativity and critical thinking in learning; (S)

(4)

to demonstrate basic understanding of ancient texts and apply the methods of reading them; (K/S)

(5)

to gain the basic knowledge of literature and its development, and develop sustained interest in further pursuing some of the literary issues; (K/V)

(6)

to discuss the differences in literary texts, and apply literary concepts and theories to analyse texts; (K/S)

(7)

to express his or her ideas in a precise manner; (S)

(8)

to identify grammatical properties of Chinese and English in a linguistically informed manner; (K)

(9)

to describe and compare the similarities and differences between Chinese and English without prejudice, and be able to use this knowledge to produce high- quality translations; (K/S)

(10)

to synthesize the principles of comparative grammar and theorize about the linguistic facts, and be able to apply this knowledge in translation practice; (K)

(11)

to develop sustained interest in further pursuing some of the linguistic issues arisen from comparative grammar; (V)

(12)

to understand and evaluate translations across a wide range of genres, including literary, legal, business and media texts; (K/S)

(13)

to produce effective translations, using appropriate strategies in a variety of contexts; (K/S)

(14)

to provide effective consecutive or simultaneous interpretation, using appropriate strategies in a variety of contexts; (K/S)

(15)

to be able to conduct independent critical reflection of translations in the form of written and oral commentary; (K/S/V)

(16)

to understand basic concepts of translation and apply these concepts to the production and evaluation of translations; (K/V)

(17)

to understand theoretical orientations in translation studies in their cultural and historical contexts, and use appropriate theoretical models in the production and evaluation of translations; (K/V)

(18)

to understand the role of translators and interpreters in the dissemination of knowledge, the construction of identity, and the negotiation of power relations; and (K/V)

(19)

to develop intercultural awareness, a lifelong interest in learning, and a love for the richness of human expression. (K/V)

 

 

K = Knowledge outcomes        S = Skills outcomes         V = Values and attitude outcomes

 

 

3.

Minor Programme:

 

Upon completion of the Minor Programme, students will have demonstrated basic understanding of fundamental issues related to the subject, acquired knowledge of some aspects of the subject with sufficient exposure and at a suitable level to allow further study in this subject or related subjects or to pursue a related career.
